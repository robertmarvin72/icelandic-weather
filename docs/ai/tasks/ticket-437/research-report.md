### F-02 — Weather-voice's `windMax` silently switches basis (time-weighted vs. raw daily max), producing opposite user-facing tone for the identical wind event (HIGH impact, VERIFIED, reproduced live)

`forecastNormalize.js` computes `windMax` as `Math.max` of `(rawHourlyWind × timeWeight)` across a day's hours **when hourly data is present** — this qualifier matters (added in v2, R5): the time-of-day weighting (0.45 for 00-06h, 0.75 for 06-09h/22-24h, 1.0 for 09-22h, floored at 0.85 if `rawWind>=22 OR rawGust>=28`) only applies on that branch. When hourly data is absent, `windMax` falls back to the raw, UNweighted `windspeed_10m_max` daily field instead. **How often the hourly-absent fallback actually occurs in production is UNKNOWN** — not measured in this research. `weatherVoiceEngine.js` receives whichever basis produced `windMax` with zero awareness of which one it was, and compares it directly against 5/10/15 m/s thresholds. Demonstrated live (reference-cases.md RC-21a/RC-21b): an identical raw 18 m/s hourly gust produces `condition:"extreme_wind", mood:"wrecked", severity:3` (the app's single most severe voice tone) when it happens to occur at a daytime hour, but only `condition:"sun_wind", mood:"suspicious", severity:1` at a nighttime hour — **a mild, sarcastic-tone result** (corrected in v2, R5: this is not the overall mildest possible tone — `good`/`excellent` conditions are severity 0 — only that it is far milder than the daytime outcome for the identical event), purely from the time-of-day weight, nothing to do with any official wind-speed definition, and invisible to the user.

**Extension (added in v2, B2 — J4 case 1, previously not assessed)**: `src/i18n/weatherVoice/safety.js`'s copy, next to the Beaufort table and its actual triggers. IS: `"Hvassviðri er í spánni."` (cautious, fires for `10 < windMax <= 15`, time-weighted-or-raw-fallback) / `"Mjög hvassviðri er í spánni."` (serious, fires for `windMax > 15`, no upper bound). **Corrected: v1 called this "cleaner — no Beaufort-named term used." That is wrong on this research's own source register — "Hvassviðri" (without "Mjög") IS Beaufort row 8, 17,2-20,7 m/s (source-register.md SRC-01, full table).**

**Precise arithmetic (corrected in v3, N2 — v2's "6-9 m/s short" claim was imprecise and partly wrong, replaced here with exact inequalities rather than silently restated)**: the cautious trigger's range is `10 < windMax <= 15`, so its deficit from row 8's 17,2 m/s lower bound, `17,2 - windMax`, lies in **[2,2, 7,2)** — smaller windMax values near the 10 boundary are further below row 8 (deficit near 7,2), values near the 15 boundary are closer to it (deficit near 2,2). The serious trigger's range is `windMax > 15` with **no upper bound** — it is NOT confined below row 8 at all: for `15 < windMax < 17,2`, the deficit is in **(0, 2,2)** (still below row 8, by at most 2,2 m/s); but for `windMax >= 17,2`, "Mjög hvassviðri" fires for a value that is AT or ABOVE row 8's own start — including arbitrarily high values that would themselves fall in Stormur/Rok/Ofsaveður/Fárviðri territory (rows 9-12) or beyond. So: the cautious copy always undershoots row 8 by a bounded, qualitatively described amount (2,2-7,2 m/s); the serious copy sometimes undershoots it by a small amount (up to 2,2 m/s) and sometimes does not undershoot it at all — "Mjög hvassviðri," on its own, does not correspond to any single bounded severity range, official or otherwise, and must not be read as implying one.

"Mjög hvassviðri" is not itself one of the 13 official Beaufort names; it is the app's own intensified variant of the row-8 name. **Grade, per J4's required framing: term/colloquial ambiguity, not an error** — "hvassviðri" plausibly carries an everyday, non-Beaufort-specific sense too (nr/936 demonstrates this general dual-sense pattern for "ofsaveður" — INFERRED by analogy for "hvassviðri" itself, since no source directly confirms an everyday sense for that specific word). The EN strings ("Strong winds are forecast." / "Very strong winds are forecast.") do not themselves use a Beaufort-named English word — only the IS strings do; neither EN string implies a Beaufort mean either, since the underlying `windMax` is not asserted to be one (see F-06). Exposure: VERIFIED live, today's row, home page only (`App.jsx:310` → `useForecast.js:158` → `useWeatherVoice.js:166-170`, independently confirmed by Jonesy's own re-check).

**Scale of the ambiguous `windMax` basis (added in v3, N1)**: this same time-weighted-vs-raw-daily-max basis-switch (the core of F-02) is not confined to the weather-voice copy above. Tracing the forecast-table and recommendation surfaces named in N1 found it is also the quantity behind: the main homepage forecast table's wind column (inventory.md INV-A09 — arguably the single most prominently displayed wind figure in the app), that table's gust-badge visibility gate at a newly-found 15 m/s cutoff (INV-A10/INV-T16), the Weather-Finder "avg wind" ranking display (INV-T15, v2), and the homepage's own canonical stay/move/consider decision surface's wind comparison (INV-A14/INV-T17, a 1.5 m/s "meaningfully better" difference threshold, deliberately independent of every other threshold in this register per that file's own comment). Counting all of these, the ambiguous basis has at least 8-9 independent consumers across the app, not just the one (voice) this research originally traced.

### F-03 — `hazardWindow`'s "storm" family label is duration-gated, not magnitude-gated, and fires identically from rain alone with zero wind (MEDIUM-HIGH impact, VERIFIED, reproduced live, reachable on a real surface)

`detectHazardWindow`'s `isHazardHour` = `wind>=14 OR gust>=20 OR rain>=12` (raw hourly); the resulting `"passingStorm"`/`"roughWeather"`/`"stormyPeriod"` label depends ONLY on how many CONSECUTIVE hazard-hours occurred (1-2h / 3-5h / 6+h), never on which condition fired or by how much it exceeded its threshold. Demonstrated live (reference-cases.md RC-15a vs RC-15b): 6 hours of qualifying rain alone, with zero wind and zero gust, produces the exact same `{"type":"stormyPeriod","factor":1.6}` as 6 hours of wind at the app's own windWarn (14 m/s — itself only Beaufort-7 "Allhvass vindur", well below the official Beaufort-9 "Stormur" 20.8-24.4 m/s per source-register.md SRC-01). RC-15c shows the inverse: genuine Beaufort-Stormur-magnitude wind (20.8 m/s) sustained for only 2 hours produces the MILDEST label, `"passingStorm"`. The resulting copy says outright "A longer stormy period is expected in this window" (`translations.routePlanner.js:245-247`, EN — **corrected in v2, R1: v1 attributed this English sentence to the IS lines at `:598-600`, which are in fact the Icelandic text, "Lengra stormaskeið er líklegt á þessu tímabili."**); the severest IS tier uses "stormaskeið" (etymologically close to the Beaufort-defined "Stormur"). This surface is **VERIFIED live-reachable** — `getHazardWindowNarrative()` renders in the real, non-preview `RoutePlannerDetailsModal.jsx` (confirmed in this session's own earlier browser-evidence work on ticket #440), not a fixture. **Also now found publicly marketed** (added in v2, B3): the landing page's `landingAdvisorMoveExtra` string uses the identical "stormaskeið"/"storm period" wording as a static demo line (inventory.md INV-A02) — this phrasing is not confined to a live-computed in-app output.

**Grading per J4's required framing**: this is a **term/colloquial ambiguity**, not a stated factual error — "stormy"/"storm period" read in ordinary English/Icelandic as duration-based weather-episode descriptions, and the code's actual behavior is internally consistent with that colloquial reading; the ambiguity is that the same words also carry a specific, different, magnitude-defined Beaufort meaning in Icelandic meteorological usage, and nothing in the UI distinguishes which sense is meant. (v1 contrasted this with `safety.js`'s copy as "cleaner"; that contrast is corrected in F-02 above — `safety.js` turns out to have its own, different term/threshold tension, not a clean case.)

### F-04 — If every sampled Route Planner point's forecast fetch fails, the result is reported as "LOW" risk, indistinguishable from a genuinely calm route (MEDIUM impact, VERIFIED, reproduced live)

`estimateRouteRisk` initializes `maxWind`/`maxGust` to `0` and only updates them inside the per-point loop; if `getForecast` returns null/fails for every sampled point, the loop's `continue` means those initializers are never touched. Demonstrated live (reference-cases.md RC-17): calling the real function with a `getForecast` that always resolves to `null` returns `{"routeRisk":"LOW","maxWind":0,"maxGust":0,"rainMax":0}` — structurally identical to a confirmed-calm result. Nothing in the return shape distinguishes "we don't know" from "we checked and it's calm."

### F-05 — Five independent zero-default patterns treat missing/non-numeric wind data as calm, not as unknown (MEDIUM impact, VERIFIED)

Four were named in the research contract, a fifth was found during this research:
1. `routeRisk.js:51-53` — `windRaw ?? 0` / `gustRaw ?? 0` / `rainRaw ?? 0`.
2. `hazardWindow.js:14-16` — `typeof winds[i] === "number" ? winds[i] : 0`. Demonstrated live (RC-16): malformed hourly input (`null`, `"x"`, `undefined`, `NaN`) silently produces "no hazard window detected," the same as two genuinely-calm hours.
3. `forecastNormalize.js:55-57` — `Number(winds[j] ?? 0)`, feeding the time-weighted `windMax` in F-02.
4. `routeRisk.js` all-points-failed path — see F-04.
5. **Newly found, not in the original four**: `scoring.js:57` — `const v = w ?? 0;` inside `windPenaltyPoints`, a 5th independent zero-default feeding the main site score directly.

Each of these, independently, pulls its respective classification toward a falsely reassuring "safe" outcome whenever the underlying wind reading is missing or malformed, rather than surfacing uncertainty. Findings only, per the research contract — no fix proposed or made.

### F-01 — Main forecast path requests a wind-unit parameter this repo's own tests call "legacy"; a defense-in-depth gap, not a confirmed active bug (re-graded and demoted in v2 — LOW-MEDIUM impact, VERIFIED code / VERIFIED SAMPLE that the legacy name works on the public host / UNKNOWN for the keyed host)

**v1 said this was HIGH impact, UNKNOWN provider behavior, and the single highest-priority item — that was wrong, per Jonesy's B1, and is corrected here, not silently dropped.** The aurora pipeline (`api/_lib/auroraDecision/openMeteo.js`) requests **`wind_speed_unit=ms`** — the name Open-Meteo's own public documentation (source-register.md SRC-04) lists as canonical, with a runtime cross-check (`windUnitIsSuspect()`) that reads the response's own `hourly_units.windspeed_10m` and nulls the value if it isn't exactly `"m/s"`. The ENTIRE main forecast path — `src/lib/forecastCache.js`, proxied by `api/forecast.js`, and therefore everything downstream of it (routeRisk, hazardWindow, scoring.js, weatherVoiceRules via forecastNormalize, the whole Route Planner) — requests **`windspeed_unit=ms`** (no underscore) instead, with **no runtime unit cross-check at all** (grepped, zero matches). This repo's own test file (`api/_lib/auroraDecision/openMeteo.test.js:26,61,276`) explicitly calls `wind_speed_unit` the "documented canonical" parameter and says a "legacy `windspeed_unit` param must never be sent alongside/instead of" it.

**What v1 missed and this round found**: a retained live sample already in the repository from ticket #450 (source-register.md SRC-07, `docs/ai/tasks/ticket-450/samples/openmeteo-baseline-4point.json`) — a real request to the **public** `api.open-meteo.com` host using exactly `windspeed_unit=ms` (the legacy name), retrieved 2026-10-19. All four returned points' `hourly_units`/`daily_units` for both wind and gust are **"m/s"**. **The legacy name WAS honoured on the public host at that point in time — VERIFIED SAMPLE, not UNKNOWN.** The scenario v1 led with (km/h-scale values silently compared against m/s thresholds, "a severe, systemic over-sensitivity bug") did not happen there. What remains genuinely open is narrower: (a) the **keyed** host (`customer-api.open-meteo.com`, used when `OPEN_METEO_API_KEY` is set, per `api/_lib/openMeteoAuth.js` — traced in this session's own earlier #453 work) was not covered by that sample — UNKNOWN; (b) behavior after 2026-10-19 is unverified; (c) the main path still has no runtime `hourly_units` guard the way aurora does — a real defense-in-depth gap, VERIFIED, but a gap is not itself evidence of an active bug. The test comment calling `windspeed_unit` "legacy" is a #452-era design rule for the aurora module specifically, not evidence that the provider actually rejects or mishandles it elsewhere.

**Precise Production framing (added in v3, N4)**: `api/_lib/openMeteoAuth.js` routes to the keyed host whenever `OPEN_METEO_API_KEY` is configured, and when `VERCEL_ENV==="production"` with no key present it **fails closed with no fallback to the public host at all** (confirmed directly from that file's own logic in this session's earlier #453 work; Jonesy's v1 result review briefly asserted the opposite — a "public-host retry" after keyed failure — and corrected that in its own v2 re-check, agreeing with Ripley's final assessment that no such retry exists). The retained SRC-07 sample is **public-host evidence only**: it describes what the public host did on 2026-10-19, which is the path actually taken in a non-production environment, or in a misconfigured production that would otherwise fail closed — it is **not** proof of current production behavior whenever a key IS actually configured there. Whether a key is in fact configured in the live production environment, and what the keyed host currently returns, are operational/configuration facts this research did not and cannot independently verify by reading the repository — they are owner-reported facts, not confirmed by CC. This is the concrete, narrow reason the O-1 observation (below) is worth doing, not a generic caution.

**Corrected conclusion**: this is now a parameter-name inconsistency plus a missing defense-in-depth guard on the main path — worth a cheap owner-side check (see Recommendations, O-1), but not the headline risk v1 made it out to be, and ranked below F-02/F-03 above because those are independently reproduced, live behaviors, while this one's worst-case scenario has direct evidence against it occurring on the public host.

### F-06 — Veðurstofan's own sources disagree on the averaging basis for wind-speed classification, and Open-Meteo's hourly field is a third, different basis again (MEDIUM impact, VERIFIED)

Source-register.md SRC-01 (nr/349, 2006) states the Beaufort-based classification tables use a 10-minute mean and explicitly exclude gusts from the Beaufort scale. SRC-02 (nr/1098, 2007) states the averaging basis has historically varied by country/era (1-minute, 10-minute, or hourly) and does **not** confirm 10-minute as the current universal standard — a genuine tension between the two official sources, not resolved by this research, retained as two separate attributed claims per the binding clarification. **Narrowed somewhat in v2**: source-register.md SRC-08 (added, a retained #450 Veðurstofan AWS-hour parameter catalog) confirms Veðurstofan's OWN current standard observation field, `f`, IS explicitly a 10-minute average — direct primary-source support for the "10-minute mean" side of the tension as current PRACTICE (not just a 2006 article's claim), though it does not fully resolve nr/1098's broader "varies by country/era" caveat for every context. Separately and independently, Open-Meteo's own documented `wind_speed_10m` (hourly) is **"Instant"** — an instantaneous reading, not any kind of mean at all (SRC-04). So even with SRC-08's narrowing, the app's hourly wind field (and anything derived from it, including daily-max-of-hourly-instantaneous-values) is on a THIRD, different time-basis from Veðurstofan's own 10-minute-mean observation standard. Comparing any of this app's thresholds (14/18/20/24, or the official 20.8/24.5/28.5/32.7 Beaufort boundaries) against the app's raw/time-weighted values conflates fundamentally different time-bases, independent of which specific numeric threshold is chosen.

### F-07 — The app's "gust" field and Veðurstofan's own gust definition share the same 1-hour aggregation window, but differ in a more specific way: stated underlying sample duration (MEDIUM impact, VERIFIED, corrected in v2)

**v1's framing conflated two separate things — corrected per the approved correction prompt's explicit instruction not to treat all time-basis differences as the same kind of difference.** Open-Meteo's `wind_gusts_10m` (hourly) is documented as "Preceding hour max" (SRC-04) — the maximum reading Open-Meteo's own pipeline recorded somewhere within the hour before the timestamp; Open-Meteo's docs do not state the underlying SAMPLE duration that feeds that hourly max. Source-register.md SRC-08 (added in v2, a retained #450 primary-source catalog) gives Veðurstofan's own `fg` field precisely: "Wind gust (highest 3-sec value during past hour)" — **note that `fg` ALSO uses a "during past hour" aggregation window, structurally the same 1-hour-window-maximum as Open-Meteo's `wind_gusts_10m`.** The genuinely supported, narrower difference is: Veðurstofan states its underlying sample duration explicitly (3 seconds), while Open-Meteo's docs are silent on what duration of sample feeds its own hourly gust max. (v1's removed claim, per R1/the correction prompt: a speculative sentence about which direction this might bias readings — "can be lower than sharp bursts… or can appear to linger" — is dropped here as unsupported speculation; the only claim retained is the stated-vs-unstated sample-duration difference.)
