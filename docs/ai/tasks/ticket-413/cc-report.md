### What was wrong, and the exact fix

**1. §5.4 conflated the app's own internal hazard thresholds with officially issued external warnings.** The original text called `HAZARDS_V1`-derived notices "opinber viðvörunar-yfirborð" (official warning surfaces) — CC's own report and Jonesy's Round 1 review repeated this same attribution without catching it. `HAZARDS_V1` is an internal, app-authored threshold set; it confers no official authority, and the app has no integration with any external authority's (Veðurstofan, almannavarnir) warning feed today. Rewrote §5.4 to explicitly separate "innri hazard-tilkynningar appsins" from "opinberar viðvaranir frá utanaðkomandi yfirvöldum" as two distinct categories, state plainly that no external feed integration exists or is being invented, and require that Weather Voice safety-routing not replace or diminish *either* category. Related generic references to "viðvörunar-yfirborð" elsewhere in the document (§5.3, §11's acceptance-test list) were qualified as "(innra)" for consistency.

**2. §11 (points 3 and 4) overstated independence and omitted real severity filtering.** The original text said the selector "velur einfaldlega hvaða eligible+available línu sem er... óháð `severity`" (selects regardless of severity) and that the hazard system is "algjörlega ótengt" (completely unrelated) to Weather Voice. Both were checked against live source and found inaccurate: `weatherVoiceSelector.js`'s `isEligible()` (lines 26–27) and its `eligible` filter (line 104) genuinely filter by `severityMin`/`severityMax` — severity is not ignored. Separately, `weatherVoiceRules.js` imports `HAZARDS_V1` (line 13) and sets `RAIN_HEAVY_MM = HAZARDS_V1.rainWarn` (line 54), which `weatherVoiceEngine.js` consumes directly at line 102 to evaluate the `heavy_rain` condition — a real, verified data-flow connection, not "completely unrelated." Rewrote both points to state the accurate, more nuanced claim: severity filtering exists but is an expressive-intensity gate, not a safety gate (it has no SAFE/POOR/DANGEROUS concept and cannot stop sarcastic content from being eligible at severity 3); and a single numeric threshold is genuinely shared between the rules module and `HAZARDS_V1`, but no safety-*classification result* is computed anywhere and fed into Weather Voice selection — sharing one number is not the same as a safety signal existing. Re-verified all four cited line numbers (13, 54, 102, 26–27, 104) directly against current source before writing the correction.

**3. §5.7 and §9 let situation classification and per-message tone collapse into the same thing.** §5.7 originally listed "handvirkt authored-svið á hverri línu" (a manually authored per-line field) as a candidate *source* for the real situation's DANGEROUS classification, while §9's `voice_level` note said the SAFE/POOR/DANGEROUS names and the SARCASTIC/CAUTIOUS/SERIOUS tone names were "notaðar til skiptis" (used interchangeably) for "the same three stages." Both statements let an authored property of a message (its intended tone) stand in for, or become evidence of, the real weather situation's actual danger — exactly the collapse Ripley's review warned against, since a required one-directional mapping (situation classification constrains which tone is allowed) is not the same as the two concepts being identical or substitutable. Rewrote §5.7 to explicitly state that authoring `voice_level: serious` on one line is never evidence or authority for the actual situation being DANGEROUS, and removed the authored field as a candidate signal source (the signal source for the real situation remains genuinely unresolved — `HAZARDS_V1`, a new hazard read, or an official feed, only). Rewrote §9's `voice_level` table cell to describe it plainly as "authored eigind SKILABOÐSINS, ekki flokkun AÐSTÆÐNANNA" (an authored property of the message, not a classification of the situation) and dropped the "used interchangeably" framing. Added a new clarifying paragraph directly under §4's table making the same one-directional-mapping point at first use, and reworded §10's editorial checklist step 3 so the editor determines the situation classification for the context first (an editorial judgment, not something the line itself proves) before assigning a matching `voice_level`.

**4. §7 and §11.6 drew conclusions the current evidence doesn't support.** §7 said, in effect, that since no safety pathway exists today, the "never suppress a serious warning for lack of a joke" concern "is not a practical concern in practice" — backwards: the absence of a safety pathway is exactly *why* the concern is real and unaddressed today, not a reason it can be set aside. §11 point 6 said sarcasm in the `wind_extreme_*` entries proves the entries "fail" the new safety rules — but §6 itself correctly states `extreme_wind` has not been classified DANGEROUS by anything, so no rule violation can yet be proven either way; sarcasm in these entries is evidence of the *routing gap* (nothing today would stop it if the situation were classified DANGEROUS), not evidence the classification has already been made and breached. Also corrected §6's closing claim that `extreme_wind`/`wrecked`/severity-3 is "ekki flokkað sem DANGEROUS af neinu kerfi í dag" (not classified DANGEROUS by *any* system today) — that oversteps into claiming a fact about every other subsystem in the app (e.g. `HAZARDS_V1`'s own independent `windWarn`/`windHigh` thresholds); narrowed the claim to Weather Voice specifically. Rewrote §7 to state plainly that the missing safety flow is the *reason* §5's requirements exist, not proof they're unneeded. Rewrote §11.6 to state compliance is genuinely unverified in either direction (neither proven compliant nor proven non-compliant), and that the real, confirmed fact is the routing-gap itself, not a premature classification claim.
